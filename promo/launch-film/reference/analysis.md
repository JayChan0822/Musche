The reference’s polish comes from disciplined framing and continuity, not dense decoration. Warm white space surrounds rounded real windows; deep soft shadows establish depth. ## Translate to Musche

1. **A persistent hero object.** Introduce a real task card, then keep that same title, metadata and color while it changes from a free card to an item in the task pool, into a calendar appointment, and eventually into a recording track. This creates one continuous product story.
2. **Real UI decomposition.** Separate the window shell, date toolbar, sidebar, time grid, task cards, duration handles, track labels, status dots and logo. Animate their positions and clipping independently. Screenshots may substantiate the implementation, but should not be the animated scene’s sole surface.
3. **Window choreography.** Begin with a compact task panel, expand width to reveal the weekly grid, grow one day column for the recording detail, then pull back into the full workspace. Animate bounds and internal layout together so the product reflows rather than stretching.
4. **Action → visible result.** A click on week/month changes the calendar; dragging a task plants it into a time slot; stretching its bottom edge changes its duration; moving between project tracks reveals their organization. Every cursor gesture earns a functional response.
5. **Restrained elasticity.** Task cards may overshoot 2–4% and settle in 180–300 ms. Their shadows deepen during lift and close on landing. Small neighboring cards can react with a delayed 2–5 px movement. Use crisp, strongly eased camera travel and 1.2–2.4 second reading holds.
6. **Motivated transitions.** Match-cut a task card’s colored tab to the selected date marker; let calendar lines extend into recording track lines; use a panel edge as a wipe; use an object crossing close to camera as a short occlusion transition.
7. **Frame contrast.** Alternate one macro control, one medium interaction, one wide composition. The provided film does this repeatedly; a continuous wide calendar shot would miss its main strength.
8. **A single finish.** Return all product layers to one complete workspace, then let those layers peel outward and contract toward the real Musche mark. Hold the final wordmark and URL for at least 2.4 seconds.

One beat = 0.6 s; one 4-beat bar = 2.4 s; the film spans exactly 20 bars. Most large scene changes occur on a bar downbeat. Small details enter on eighth-note subdivisions (0.3 s), with 0.06–0.12 s local stagger when a cascade needs more texture.
